## Intended use
Teach why a high test score can be misleading, and flag email text that looks like spam or phishing with the terms behind the score.

**Out of scope:** Filtering a real mailbox; judging attachments, images, headers or senders; non-English email; any decision about a person.

## Training data
Enron-Spam (ham: Enron staff email 1999 to 2002; spam: 2001 to 2005), plus 100 synthetic modern emails (darkknight25, MIT) trained at low weight and the owner's own emails if present. Words that merely identify the Enron source are masked.

Sizes: train 21,554, val 4,521, test 4,521, external_2026_spam 10,228, challenge 34. Details: `docs/dataset_cards/`.

On the held-out half of a small synthetic modern dataset, 15 of 52 legitimate emails were flagged and 53 of 56 phishing emails were caught.

## Known limitations
- Trained on Enron-Spam (ham: Enron staff email 1999-2002; spam: 2001-2005). It has never seen a modern legitimate email in training.
- The 2026 spam test has no legitimate email, so it measures detection rate only, not false alarms.
- Only 34 hand-written modern emails (24 legitimate) check false alarms on modern mail; that is a smoke test, not a measurement.
- 100 of the extra modern training emails come from a synthetic one-sentence dataset (darkknight25); they teach modern topics but not realistic email structure.
- Part of 2026 spam is not English; the model reads English only.
- Reads only the first 6,000 characters of the body; attachments and images are ignored.

## Ethical considerations
Wrongly hiding a legitimate email costs more than letting spam through, so the threshold targets high precision. The model treats ordinary modern automated mail (statements, verification codes, receipts) as suspicious about a quarter of the time. The Email tab shows this as a standing warning. Links in emails are never opened.
