## Intended use
Show multi-label classification and per-label thresholds, and prompt a human moderator to look at a comment, with the words behind each score.

**Out of scope:** Automatic moderation, banning, ranking or reporting people; judging non-English text; anything where a false flag harms someone. Not suitable as the only moderation signal.

## Training data
Jigsaw Toxic Comment Classification (Wikipedia talk-page comments, about 2017; crowd labels). A round of synthetic neutral identity sentences was tried to reduce bias; a later round was rejected because it did not help.

Sizes: train 115,238, val 23,892, test 23,892, official_test 63,908. Details: `docs/dataset_cards/`.

Rare labels (threat, severe_toxic, identity_hate) are weaker. **Bias probe:** neutral sentences such as 'I am a ... person' were flagged up to 50% of the time for the worst identity term ('gay'). **Context probe:** 5 of 24 benign sentences that merely mention or negate toxic words were flagged.

## Known limitations
- Trained on Wikipedia talk-page comments (about 2017); slang, platforms and targets have changed.
- Labels are crowd-sourced and noisy; rare labels (threat, identity_hate, severe_toxic) have few examples, so their scores are unstable.
- A word-based model cannot read context: quotes, sarcasm, reclaimed words and discussions about toxicity can be misjudged.
- Identity terms can raise scores even in harmless sentences (see the bias probe).
- English only.

## Ethical considerations
This is the highest-risk model in the project. It can mistake the mention of an identity (for example 'gay', 'black') for toxicity and cannot read negation, quotes or sarcasm. Using it to moderate people automatically would harm exactly the groups it misjudges. The app shows the bias result as a warning on the Comments tab.
